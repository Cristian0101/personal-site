01 / DEFINE THE MARKET + FIRST SEGMENT

TAM Segmentation
Worksheet

Choose a market you can explain, a segment you can serve, and a short list worth researching.

Market universeAll organizations with the relevant need inside your stated boundary. Total addressable market (TAM) describes its full potential demand, often in annual revenue.

Serviceable segmentThe part your product and team can serve today. Apply real limits such as supported regions, integrations, and company size.

Working account listThe accounts you can responsibly research and work now, after fit, ownership, and contact checks. A list is not a forecast of wins or revenue.

1. Write rules you can actually check

Start with business needs and observable company facts. Mark unknowns for research. Do not use protected or sensitive personal traits.

2. Separate account fit from contact role

Account fit asks whether the company belongs in your segment. A contact persona describes a work role: who deals with the problem and what they own. A title alone does not prove authority or budget.

For interview practice: use a fictional scenario or public company facts. Label the finished work “practice sample.” Do not contact real prospects for the exercise or present it as client work.

02 / ESTIMATE A RANGE YOU CAN DEFEND

Keep the estimate honest.

A useful first estimate explains what was counted, where it came from, and which assumptions could change it.

Bottom-up annual market estimate
Eligible buying units × annual revenue per unit

Use the full defined market for TAM. If you count only your first segment, label the result “segment estimate.” Neither is a sales target or a forecast.

Fictional arithmetic example: 800-1,200 eligible companies × USD 900-1,500 per company per year gives USD 720,000-1,800,000 in annual potential revenue. These are invented scenario bounds, not market statistics or a confidence interval. They assume every eligible company buys.

What does this estimate cover? Choose one.

3. Keep a small evidence log

For each input, record the source URL or approved internal reference, its data/publication date, and your check date. Keep private references inside approved company systems.

03 / PRIORITIZE A SMALL ACCOUNT LIST

Decide what to check next.

Start with fit and evidence. News alone does not prove need, budget, or buying intent.

5. Try three fictional accounts

Scenario: a maintenance-scheduling tool for equipment repair shops with 10-75 employees, in your supported region, using a compatible inventory system. Everything in this exercise is invented.

Which account needs research first, and which should wait?
AccountCompany evidenceContact / gap
A35-person repair shop. Current company page confirms supported region and compatible system.Operations role found; scheduling ownership unknown.
B55-person repair shop in supported region. Old directory listing; system unknown.Relevant role not yet verified.
C140-person repair shop in supported region. Recent expansion news.Operations role found; company is outside size range.

Suggested reasoning: A: verify the role and ownership first. B: update company evidence and check the required system before outreach. C: exclude from this segment or ask your manager about another segment. An exciting signal does not override a fit rule.

6. Build your first three research rows

Use account names only, not personal contact details. Record source + date, mark unknowns, and choose research / ready for team review / hold / exclude.

Before an account becomes reachable: check territory ownership, duplicates, customers and active deals, opt-outs, and approved channels. A publicly listed contact is not permission to contact them. Follow your team's policy and local requirements.
