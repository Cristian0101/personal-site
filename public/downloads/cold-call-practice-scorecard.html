01 / SET UP A FICTIONAL CALL

Cold Call
Practice Scorecard

Practice a short, honest conversation. Listen to the answer, respect the boundary, and choose a next step that fits.

For early SDRs, BDRs, and job seekers. Use a partner or rehearse aloud on your own. No real calls, prospect details, or outreach required.

Fictional brief. You represent RelayNote, a shared handoff log with task owners, status, and notes. Mina runs operations at Harbor Hire, an equipment-rental company with shift teams. All names and details are invented for this exercise.

What you do not know: whether handoffs are a problem, what tools Mina uses, or whether a meeting would help. Pricing, integrations, customer results, and savings are not supplied. Do not invent them or imply you have spoken before.

Try a short opener, then wait

“Hi Mina, I'm [name] with RelayNote. This is an unexpected sales call. Is it okay if I briefly explain why I called?”

If invited to continue: name the handoff log, connect it to Mina's operations role, and ask how open tasks move between shifts. Treat possible relevance as a question, not a known problem.

Partner: choose one branch for this round

No recording is needed. If you record a partner, ask before recording and agree on storage and deletion. Ask separately before sharing it. Label any saved work as a fictional role-play.

02 / REVIEW OBSERVABLE BEHAVIORS

Score what happened.

After the round, choose one anchor per observed row. All scores start blank. Leave a row blank if that moment never happened, and explain why on page 3. A clean early exit may leave discovery unobserved.

Use the anchors, not a total. These are practice prompts, not a validated assessment, hiring prediction, or performance threshold. A meeting is not required for a useful round. Review actions and words, not accent, personality, or confidence.

1. Identity + relevant reason
2. Permission + room to answer
3. Discovery + listening
4. Accurate understanding
5. Respect for a boundary
6. Appropriate next step + record

Need a replay? Use the same brief and branch so the change is easier to observe. A solo rehearsal cannot show how you respond to a real partner; leave those rows blank.

03 / RETRY ONE MOMENT

Make the feedback usable.

Write down a specific moment. Change one response, then try it again. Keep the useful parts in your own words.

Choose the next step from the conversation

If there is a relevant issue and willingness: offer a short discussion with a clear purpose and person. Ask if it would be useful before agreeing a time and time zone. Do not promise unverified results.

If there is no interest, no fit, or a request to stop: a respectful ending is the next step. “Thanks for letting me know. I'll end here.” For a no-contact request, note it for the approved process. Do not switch channels to get around it.

Run the retry

  1. Pick one rubric row. Rewrite only the line or behavior you want to change.
  2. Replay that moment using the same branch. Ask the partner to answer naturally.
  3. Compare the observable behavior. If it is still unclear, make the change smaller.
