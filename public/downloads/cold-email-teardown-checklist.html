01 / AUDIT THE REASON TO WRITE

Cold Email
Teardown Checklist

Find the reason to write. Remove the claims you cannot support. Make the next step useful and easy to understand.

For early SDRs, BDRs, and job seekers. Work through the fictional example, then rewrite it on page 3. No real recipients, contact lists, or live sends needed.

1. Can you explain why this reader?

2. Does the draft earn the reader's attention honestly?

Before any real outreach: confirm your team's approval, contact restrictions, and applicable local rules. Public contact details do not automatically permit marketing. Keep personal data in approved systems; do not paste private prospect information into unapproved AI tools.

02 / WATCH THE EDIT HAPPEN

Same facts. Clearer email.

Fictional practice brief. All people, companies, sources, and capabilities below are invented for this exercise. No email was sent and no results are claimed.

Facts within the scenario: Mina leads onboarding at Cedar Bay IT. A sample careers post dated November 4 lists two onboarding-coordinator openings. Jules works at Relaypath, whose sample product brief describes task owners and due dates in a shared onboarding checklist. Jules has a one-page example ready to share.

Unknown: Cedar Bay's current process, whether handoffs are a problem, buying interest, and permission to contact Mina.

Before: identify the problems before reading the notes

Fictional draft with deliberate errors

From: Your onboarding team
Subject: Re: our chat

Hi Mina, I loved your recent interview. With your hiring surge, your handoffs must be a mess. Relaypath is the industry-leading platform that guarantees effortless onboarding. Can you book a demo tomorrow, review our deck, and introduce me to your boss? We need to move fast. Jules

After: supported facts, one possible issue, one ask

Fictional body example, not ready to send

From: Jules at Relaypath <jules@relaypath.example>
Subject: Onboarding handoff checklist

Hi Mina,

Your careers post lists two onboarding-coordinator openings. If keeping handoff ownership clear is on your radar, Relaypath gives each onboarding task a named owner and due date.

Would a one-page example of the handoff checklist be useful?

Jules
Relaypath

Reserved example address, not a real sender. A live version also needs the required disclosures, sender details, and a working opt-out process. See page 3.

  1. Identity: replace a disguised sender and fake reply subject with a clear name and topic.
  2. Evidence: remove the invented interview, “hiring surge,” and assumed broken process.
  3. Relevance: connect a possible handoff issue to a capability in the brief. No guarantee.
  4. Ask: offer one specific, available example. Remove the pile of requests and false urgency.

03 / REWRITE IT. REVIEW IT.

Make every sentence useful.

Use only the facts on page 2, or a clearly labeled fictional brief of your own. Write a fresh version. Keep your explanation separate from the email.

Read it as the recipient, not the writer

A writing check is not clearance to send. For real outreach, confirm local consent and privacy requirements with your team. For U.S. commercial email, check accurate headers/subject, required advertising disclosure, valid postal address, and a clear working opt-out. Follow the applicable rules and honor opt-outs promptly; do not evade a refusal through another channel. A reply-based opt-out works only if your team actually processes it.

Further reading: FTC: U.S. commercial email guide · ICO: UK business-to-business marketing (guidance under review when checked November 11, 2026).
