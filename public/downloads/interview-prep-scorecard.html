02 / YOUR EVIDENCE

Turn a real experience
into a clear answer.

Choose one specific moment when you helped a customer, handled a setback, organized competing tasks, or used feedback. Paid work, volunteering, and projects can all give you useful evidence.

Keep it true. Describe your own contribution. Use numbers only when you can support them. If you do not have a metric, explain an outcome you actually observed. Do not borrow someone else's story or imply you held a sales role you did not hold.

Say it, then check it

Aim for a focused first answer, then leave room for follow-up. Can you explain “What exactly did you do?” and “How do you know what changed?” If not, add a missing fact or choose a better example.

03 / REHEARSE

Five questions.
Your words.

Write a few cues for each answer. Practice aloud rather than memorizing a script. These are practice prompts, not a promise of what an employer will ask.

1. Walk me through your background.

Current work or focus, one relevant experience, and why this role is your next step. Try a 60-90 second first take; adjust to the interview format.

2. Why sales, and why an SDR or BDR role?

Give your real motivation. Show that you understand prospecting, outreach, rejection, and follow-up, rather than only the job title.

3. Tell me about a time you faced rejection or a setback.

Use a true example. Explain what you tried, how you responded, and what you learned. Page 2 can help.

4. How do you manage a high volume of tasks?

Describe a real system for prioritizing, tracking, and following up. Use an example from your own work, even if it was outside sales.

5. Why this company?

Connect one verified company detail to a genuine reason you are interested. Be ready to name where you found it.

Run a short mock. Answer the five prompts, take one follow-up question, then ask your closing question. Ask your partner to note exact words or moments, not whether you “seemed confident.”
